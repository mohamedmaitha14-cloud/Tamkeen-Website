Private-Sector Labour Laws in the United Arab Emirates

Private-Sector Labour Laws in the United Arab Emirates

The United Arab Emirates has established a comprehensive legal framework to regulate employment relationships in the private sector. At the heart of this framework is Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, a pivotal element of UAE labour law with a significant impact on employers and employees alike.

This legislation plays a crucial role in shaping the rights and duties of private-sector employees, ensuring fair treatment and protecting their interests at work. Understanding its implications requires examining its key provisions and practical application. This article explores employee rights, including working hours, overtime pay and annual leave, as well as employers’ obligations, the potential consequences of non-compliance, and the law’s interaction with other UAE legislation and regulations.

Overview of UAE Labour Law: Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021

Key Provisions

Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 introduced significant changes to private-sector employment contracts. One of the most notable provisions was the transition to fixed-term contracts. As described in the original article, employment contracts were limited to a maximum term of three years, with the possibility of extension or renewal for equal or shorter periods by agreement between employer and employee.

The law also sets a probationary period for new employees, not exceeding six months from the start of employment. This probationary period may be applied only once during an individual's employment with the same company.

Scope and Applicability

The law applies broadly to private-sector employees in the UAE and covers different aspects of the employment relationship. Provisions conflicting with the decree-law are deemed invalid, even if they predate its entry into force, unless they are more favourable to the employee.

The original article states that employers were required to convert existing unlimited-term contracts into fixed-term contracts within one year of the decree-law taking effect on 2 February 2022.

Recent Amendments Described in the Original Article

The original article outlines changes intended to strengthen employee rights and clarify employer obligations:

  • Notice periods: A minimum of 14 days’ notice for termination by either the company or employee, increasing to 30 days in cases involving an employee transfer.
  • Working hours: A maximum of 48 hours per week, not exceeding 144 hours over three weeks.
  • Overtime pay: Normal hourly pay plus an increase of at least 25% for overtime. For night work between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m., the increase is stated as at least 50% of basic pay.
  • Annual leave: 30 days per year for employees with more than one year of service.
  • Sick leave: 15 days at full pay, followed by 30 days at half pay, with subsequent periods unpaid.

These changes aim to create a more balanced and equitable private-sector working environment, aligning with international labour standards while maintaining the UAE’s economic competitiveness.

Employee Rights Under the Labour Law

Non-Discrimination

The law emphasizes non-discrimination in the workplace. It prohibits discrimination based on race, colour, sex, religion, nationality, social origin or disability. The goal is to ensure equal opportunities and prevent bias in recruitment and continued employment.

Employers are prohibited from discriminating between employees performing the same duties. The principle also applies to gender: women are entitled to equal pay for equal work or work of equal value.

Fair Compensation

The law requires fair compensation practices. Employers must pay wages on their scheduled dates in accordance with systems approved by the ministry. This helps prevent late or unpaid wages and ensures employees receive the compensation due for their work.

The original article also describes protections such as preventing employers from withholding employees’ passports or identity documents, thereby safeguarding freedom of movement and reducing the risk of exploitation.

Work–Life Balance

The law addresses work–life balance by regulating working hours. The original article states a maximum of 48 hours per week and 144 hours over three weeks. It also claims that later amendments reduced this to 42 hours per week, with the possibility of a longer working week by agreement, provided overtime is compensated.

Overtime pay is described as normal hourly pay plus at least 25%, or at least 50% of basic pay for night work between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m.

Leave entitlements are also discussed. The original article states that employees with more than one year of service receive 30 days of annual leave, compared with a previously stated entitlement of 21 days. The longer leave period is intended to allow adequate rest and support wellbeing and productivity.

Employer Obligations and Compliance

Contractual Requirements

The original article describes the introduction of fixed-term contracts of up to three years, or shorter terms agreed by both parties. It states that there is no limit on the number of consecutive fixed-term contracts an employer may enter into.

It also states that employers had until February 2023 to move employees onto fixed-term agreements. Article 13 is described as setting out several employer obligations, including implementing internal workplace rules. The article notes that implementing regulations were expected to provide additional detail.

Workplace Safety Measures and Record-Keeping

To support compliance with workplace requirements, employers must maintain comprehensive employment records. The original article states that records must be retained for at least two years after the end of employment. This requirement helps promote transparency and accountability.

Wage Protection System (WPS)

The UAE has implemented the Wage Protection System (WPS) to protect employees’ rights to fair and timely payment. Under Article 16 of the implementing regulations of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, establishments registered with the ministry must pay wages on time through WPS or another ministry-approved system.

The original article states that wages become due on the first day of the month following the payday specified in the employment contract. If the contract does not specify a period, employees must be paid at least once a month. Employers are considered late if payment is not made within 15 days of the due date, unless the contract provides otherwise.

Employers must provide evidence of wage payments when requested. The original article states that an establishment is considered compliant when it transfers more than 90% of workers’ wages across employment contract types. Where lawful deductions apply, a worker is considered paid if at least 80% of the contractual wage is received, provided evidence of the deductions can be supplied upon request.

Conclusion

Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 has significantly shaped employment relationships in the UAE private sector. It establishes important provisions concerning fixed-term contracts, probation, working hours and leave entitlements. These rules aim to foster a fair and balanced working environment while protecting employees’ rights and employers’ interests.

The law’s emphasis on non-discrimination, fair compensation and work–life balance reflects the UAE’s commitment to international labour standards. By establishing clear contractual requirements, workplace safeguards and a robust wage protection system, the framework seeks to support a safer and more productive private-sector workforce. Protecting workers’ rights remains essential to the country’s economic growth.
